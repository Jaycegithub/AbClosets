    Does a Custom Closet Need a Permit in Fort Lauderdale?

    Whether a closet project needs a Fort Lauderdale building permit depends on the property type, job value, and scope of work, and the City Building Official makes the final call.

    A Florida law that took effect July 1, 2026, opened a narrow exemption for small single-family projects, but not every closet installation qualifies.

    The City of Fort Lauderdale lays out the rules on its HB 803 permitting page:

    • Eligibility: Certain work on a single-family home valued under $7,500, based on a licensed contractor’s estimate, can be exempt. Cabinet replacement and other cosmetic, non-structural work appear among the city’s examples, but a specific closet job qualifies only if its actual scope fits.
    • Approval first: The exemption is not automatic. The owner or contractor submits the Residential Permit Exemption Request form and a job cost estimate through LauderBuild, then waits for written approval before starting work.
    • Exclusions: Condominiums, apartments, multifamily buildings, and homes in flood hazard areas do not qualify, and neither do electrical, plumbing, mechanical, gas, or structural work.
    • No splitting: Breaking a larger job into smaller ones to stay under $7,500 is not allowed.
    • Other approvals: Zoning, setbacks, and HOA or association requirements still apply to exempt work.

    Closet lighting that needs new circuits counts as electrical work, so it falls outside the exemption.

    Condo owners and anyone unsure can ask the city’s Building Services division, which issues permits and runs inspections, before fabrication starts.

    How Do Humidity and Salt Air Affect Closet Materials in Fort Lauderdale?

    Coastal humidity wears down closet materials that are not sealed or ventilated. The EPA’s mold and moisture guide recommends keeping indoor relative humidity below 60%, ideally between 30% and 50%, and suggests air conditioning, dehumidifiers, and better airflow to get there.

    Four construction details decide how a closet copes with that moisture:

    • Panel core: High-density melamine generally holds its shape in damp air better than standard laminate over low-density particleboard, which can warp under load.
    • Edge sealing: Factory-sealed edges keep moisture out of the core, while open or poorly bonded edge banding lets it in and starts to peel.
    • Hardware: Corrosion-resistant hinges, glides, and rods matter most near the beach and the Intracoastal, where salt air reaches inland.
    • Airflow: Layouts that let air move, along with extra care for closets that share an exterior wall, limit trapped moisture and musty odors.

    How long any system lasts also depends on installation quality, daily use, and how well the home controls humidity, so no single lifespan figure fits every Fort Lauderdale closet.

    How to Choose a Closet Company in Fort Lauderdale

    Choosing a Fort Lauderdale closet company comes down to checking who cuts the panels, who installs them, and what the paperwork promises. Each check takes minutes and protects a purchase that stays in the home for years.

    • License status: Use the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation’s license verification tool to look up the company or its contractor by name or license number.
    • Production and crew: Ask where they cut panels and whether installers are employees or subcontractors.
    • Itemized pricing: Request a quote that lists panel material, drawer construction, hardware, and labor line by line, which makes comparing closet quotes straightforward.
    • Warranty and service: Ask for the warranty document, what it covers, and who handles repair calls after installation.
    • Change orders: Ask how the price changes if final measurements or wall conditions differ from the proposal.
    • Schedule in writing: Get the fabrication window and installation date on paper.
    • Cancellation rights: The FTC Cooling-Off Rule can allow cancellation until midnight of the third business day for qualifying sales of $25 or more made at home, including invited presentations. Exclusions apply, so whether a given contract qualifies depends on how and where it was finalized.

    The same checklist applies anywhere in Broward County, from Hollywood to Deerfield Beach, because the risks of split responsibility don’t change at the city line.

    Frequently Asked Questions

    How much does a custom closet cost in Fort Lauderdale?

    A custom closet in Fort Lauderdale typically starts near $1,200 for a basic reach-in, while a fully featured walk-in runs about $4,500 to $8,000 or more. The final figure comes from an itemized quote after in-home measurement, since drawers, hardware, and lighting change the total.

    How long does it take to install a custom closet?

    Installing a custom closet usually takes one working day once panels are cut. Large or luxury walk-ins can need a second day. Consultation, design approval, and fabrication come first, so the whole project takes longer.

    Does a closet system need a permit in Fort Lauderdale?

    A closet system in Fort Lauderdale may need a building permit depending on scope, and the city decides. Some single-family, non-structural jobs under $7,500 can receive a written HB 803 exemption, but condos, flood-zone homes, and new electrical work are excluded.

    Can a custom closet be installed in a Fort Lauderdale condo?

    Yes. Custom closets fit Fort Lauderdale condos, including high-rise units near Las Olas, with compact reach-in layouts and vertical storage ideas for condo closet planning. Installers must coordinate work hours, elevator reservations, and proof of insurance with building management.

    Is a custom closet worth it compared to wire shelving?

    A custom closet often suits Fort Lauderdale homes better than wire shelving, which can corrode at its joints in humid air. Sealed panels and corrosion-resistant hardware handle moisture more reliably, though real value still depends on materials, edge sealing, ventilation, and daily use.

    What closet materials hold up best in Fort Lauderdale humidity?

    Sealed high-density melamine and properly finished wood veneer tend to hold up better in Fort Lauderdale humidity, because both resist moisture at the surface and edges. Standard laminate over low-density particleboard can warp, and poorly bonded edge banding can peel in damp air.

    What happens after requesting a design consultation?

    After you request a consultation, a designer measures the closet in your home or reviews measurements you send by video. In Broward County, these consultations are free; the designer builds a 3D model, and the quote itemizes each cost, so the homeowner sees layout and price first.

    Does American Built-In Closets offer virtual design consultations?

    Yes. American Built-In Closets offers design consultations by video as well as in person. A virtual meeting works best when the homeowner sends accurate measurements, clear photos, and a wardrobe count, while an in-home visit lets the designer confirm dimensions and obstacles directly.

    Can a homeowner cancel a closet contract after signing it?

    Sometimes. The FTC Cooling-Off Rule covers many sales of $25 or more made at a buyer’s home, including invited visits, until midnight of the third business day. Exclusions apply, including sales finalized at a seller’s permanent business location, so each contract’s circumstances decide.
